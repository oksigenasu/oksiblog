having a power button (key) in a row with keyboard is Nightmare.  
having a dud power button on a non-removable welded keyboard? double the nightmare!  
  
This laptop suffers from weak power button. to turn it on, you need to press the power button for 20 seconds or more depending on your luck.  
  
having previously working on replacing a keyboard on the exact same laptop type made me not want to relive that nightmare again.  
  
after tried a couple of idea like fixing the keyboard membrane and plug out the keyboard backlight cable, the problem still persist.  
  
so i suggested to add new power button using jumper from the motherboard to the owner. well, more like coaxing, since i don't want to replace the keyboard.  
  
the price of the keyboard is standard, but the cost to replace it sometimes cost more, and risky. also since the keyboard is so long, replacing it without proper sonic welding might resulted to "saggy" keyboard. its a win-win i guess?  
  
picking the jumper line is easy, look up the schematic and a footprint of the power button is located on the mobo. but deciding where to put the new button? its hard.  
  
after rummaging through component toolbox and found nothing, i ask my colleague for an smd button. luckily he got a couple of the button that is a perfect fit for the kensington slot!  
  
so i place the button on the kensington slot (hoping that the owner would never use it), but a problem arise when i tried to put the bottom cover. turns out the sticking out button preventing the cover to close.  
  
heh, lets just cut the cover and glue it later using double tape. problem solved, job done.  
  
next time when there is a job to replace welded keyboard with dead power button, i will suggest this solution...
